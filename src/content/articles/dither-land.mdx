## noise makes shapes, order makes light

Last time I made [circles out of points](/the-joy-of-plotting) and messed them up with noise. Noise is great at _shapes_.

This time I wanted something noise is spectacularly bad at: a **gradient**.

The sketch fires a ray per cell into a pile of noise until it bumps into a mountain, and hands back one number per cell — how much light lands here, `0` to `1`.

The palette is three flat colours. Background, ink, accent. Nothing in between, ever. Same deal as a pen plotter: it's down or it's up, ink or paper, one bit.

So I have `0.63`, and a box containing **black** and **not black**. Softness has to come from _how I arrange the yes and the no_.

## two bad answers

Above `0.5`? Ink. Below? Nothing. Perfectly ordered, perfectly useless — a black blob, a white blob, no mountain in between.

Fine, make the threshold **random**: a cell at `0.63` turns black 63% of the time. Statistically flawless, visually analog TV static. And my sketch animates, so every frame re-rolls the dice and the image boils like a bad soup.

Order gives me blobs. Chaos gives me static.

## randomness, standing in line

A **Bayer matrix**: 64 numbers, `0` to `63`, arranged so any two neighbours sit as far apart in value as possible. It looks scattered. It's completely fixed.

Every cell gets its own threshold from a repeating 8x8 tile:

```ts
const threshold = (bayer8[(row % 8) * 8 + (column % 8)] + 0.5) / 64;
if (density[index] > threshold) {
  context.fillRect(column * cell, row * cell, cell, cell);
}
```

That's the whole trick. Neighbours hold wildly different thresholds, so they **never agree on when to flip**. Dark region, only the low numbers switch on, sprinkled apart. Brighter, more of them join in — in a fixed, fair order. Your eye, being lazy and lovely, averages the squares back into a tone that was never there.

## the loop is another circle

The sketch runs on an 8 second loop, and the sun goes round. Which is the exact trick from last time — angle in, position out — except the angle is now the clock:

```ts
const angle = playhead * tau;
const light = { x: Math.cos(angle), y: 0.78, z: Math.sin(angle) };
```

`playhead` runs `0` to `1`, that's one full turn, and the light lands exactly where it started. No jump, no stitch. (Yes, I went with `tau` this time.)

The clouds pull the same move. Drifting noise in a straight line never comes home, so instead I walk them around a tiny circle **inside the noise field** — same wobbly-circle trick as last time, drawn with time instead of geometry.

And through all of it the dither holds still, because a cell's threshold depends on _where it is_, never on when you asked.

## the accent needs its own queue

Third colour: patches of pigment weathered into the rock, decided separately from the light. Reusing the same threshold made the accent clump into exactly the same dots as the shadows — two questions, one queue, everybody answering together.

Same matrix, read at an offset:

```ts
const accent = (bayer8[((column + 3) % 8) * 8 + ((row + 5) % 8)] + 0.5) / 64;
```

Shift it, swap the axes, done.

Noise for the mountains, 64 integers for the light, a circle for the clock. Nothing here is smooth — every pixel made a hard yes/no choice, and the softness is a hallucination happening in your eyeballs.
